How MissTheBoat scores crowd pressure

Every score on this site is a modeled estimate, not a live measurement, unless a page explicitly says otherwise. We label estimates as estimates because that’s what makes the site useful to fact-check — and citable.

MissTheBoat combines two signals for each destination and day:

  • Cruise pressure — how many cruise passengers are likely passing through the nearest port(s) and heading to this destination on excursions, sized against the destination’s population.
  • Day-trip risk — for a small set of destinations with structurally severe overtourism independent of cruise traffic (San Gimignano, Positano, and roughly twenty others), a fixed adjustment reflecting known visitor-to-resident pressure.

The pressure score, in plain terms

A cruise ship docking in a city of 400,000 people (Naples) doesn’t crowd it the way the same ship docking in a town of 8,000 (San Gimignano) does. Raw ship counts or passenger counts alone miss this. So the model:

  • Estimates ship calls at the nearest port(s) for the date, by season and day of week.
  • Multiplies by an average passengers-per-ship figure to get an estimated passenger count.
  • Divides by the destination’s population to produce a pressure ratio.
  • Runs that ratio through an asymptotic curve, not a straight line — so a ratio of 40 doesn’t score twice as crowded as a ratio of 20. Crowding feels worse at the low end of the scale than the math would suggest linearly, and flattens out at the high end once a place is already overwhelmed.
  • Blends that (70% weight) with a raw day-of-week crowd index for the destination itself (30% weight), since not all crowding is cruise-driven.
  • Adds a fixed point boost for the destinations independently flagged for severe day-tripper overtourism.
  • Caps the final score at 99. Nothing on this site claims certainty.

A note on “today’s” ship counts

Select ports show a same-day arrivals check pulled from a third-party cruise schedule aggregator. This is a best-effort, real-time supplement layered on top of the seasonal model — not a substitute for it. It depends on a third party’s page structure and can fail silently if that structure changes; when it’s unavailable, the site falls back to the seasonal estimate and says so.

Why estimates, not live tracking, for most of the dataset

Live AIS ship-tracking data exists and could replace the seasonal model outright. It isn’t in production yet because of cost, not feasibility — it’s a planned upgrade, not a current claim. Until then, every figure on this site is exactly as accurate as “typical for this port, this season, this day of week” — which is enough to plan around, and is stated as such everywhere it appears.

Updates and corrections

Seasonal patterns and destination data are reviewed and refreshed monthly. Population and excursion data are corrected as better sources are confirmed. Spot a stale figure or a wrong excursion mapping? Reach out via the site — corrections land in the next monthly refresh.
